## Contextual Analysis

E2B occupies the runtime-infrastructure layer of the AI agent stack. Public references identify it as the code-execution backend for downstream platforms including Perplexity (data analysis), Hugging Face (Open R1 training runs), Manus (computer-use sandbox), Groq (compound AI inference), and Lindy (workflow automation). This is the first sandbox-runtime platform in the VERDICT index, and the dimension-by-dimension reading should be interpreted with that transitive blast radius in view: a vulnerability or supply-chain compromise affecting an `e2b` package would propagate to every agent platform that integrates the SDK, and a breach in the hosted control plane would propagate to every customer not running BYOC or self-hosted.

The Firecracker microVM primitive is inherited from AWS upstream. AWS Firecracker advisories propagate to E2B's sandbox VMs through whatever pipeline the operator maintains; that pipeline is not described in public documentation. The presence of `e2b-dev/firecracker` as a fork suggests deliberate upstream tracking, but absence of a stated patch-absorption SLA is a recorded concern rather than a security-positive signal.

The terms of service license over "Submissions" — granting FoundryLabs perpetual, royalty-free rights to derivative works for purposes including "generally operating our business" — is broad relative to peer infrastructure providers that publish explicit training-exclusion clauses. Public materials do not confirm whether customer code is or is not used in model training; the data simply does not address the question.

The privacy policy revision date (April 8, 2024) is approximately 24 months stale relative to evaluation date, while the company has materially expanded scope (Series A funding, 88% Fortune 100 sign-up claim, EU-region availability). For an infrastructure provider serving Fortune 100 enterprises, the gap between operational scope and privacy-policy currency is observable.

The three-mode network egress design (allow-all default, deny-all, user-defined rules) gives customers a path to a deny-by-default posture but requires explicit configuration. Comparable platforms (Vercel Sandbox per its public KB) take the inverse precedence — deny rules override allow rules — as the architectural default.

## Economic Risk (P dimension)

Public pricing is usage-based (per-vCPU-hour). Public documentation does not describe a customer-facing cost-cap mechanism for autonomous sandbox creation. For workloads where an agent is granted authority to spin up sandboxes without rate-limiting or budget controls, the documented surface area does not show a built-in spend ceiling. Concurrent sandbox limits per team are documented in changelog entries but operate on capacity, not spend.
