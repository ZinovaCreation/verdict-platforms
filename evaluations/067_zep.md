## Executive Summary

Zep is a Delaware-registered context engineering platform for AI agents, operating a dual-tier structure: the Apache 2.0–licensed Graphiti library (the temporal knowledge graph engine) and the closed-source Zep Cloud managed service built on top of it. The operator is SOC 2 Type 2 certified (Oneleet-attested) and markets HIPAA compliance with BAA availability, and offers four deployment models including Bring Your Own Key (AWS KMS), Bring Your Own Model, and Bring Your Own Cloud (customer VPC). One CVE (CVE-2026-32247, CVSS 8.1, Cypher injection in Graphiti) was disclosed and patched on the same day (2026.03.11) by the founder/CEO acting as project maintainer, with full GHSA advisory and credit to the external reporter. The publicly accessible privacy and data-handling documentation is materially thinner than the SOC 2/HIPAA posture would suggest: the Privacy Policy (last revised 2024.01.27) is a generic website privacy policy that does not address customer conversation content, GDPR, AI training use, sub-processors, or data retention; the DPA and sub-processor list are gated behind the trust center contact flow rather than publicly published. The score reflects strong open-source transparency and incident-handling discipline alongside limited public data-conduct disclosure under the framework's silence-is-data principle.

## Contextual Analysis

Zep operates in a category — agent memory layer with temporal knowledge graph construction — where no prior VERDICT Layer 0 evaluation exists, and the dimensional scoring reflects an independent assessment against the v0.3.1 rubric rather than a comparison against an absent category benchmark.

The evaluation surfaces a structural pattern worth recording: the operator has invested in third-party-attested security posture (SOC 2 Type 2 via Oneleet, HIPAA tier with BAA) and a sophisticated deployment matrix (Managed, BYOK, BYOM, BYOC), and the open-source Graphiti tier demonstrates strong incident-handling and supply-chain hygiene. At the same time, the publicly accessible privacy and data-handling documentation surface — the Privacy Policy v1.0 dated 2024.01.27, the absence of a public DPA, and the absence of a public sub-processor list — is materially thinner than the SOC 2/HIPAA posture would suggest, with material data-handling disclosures gated behind the trust center contact flow. Under VERDICT's silence-is-data principle, absence in publicly accessible documentation is scored as zero regardless of what is available under contract. This produces a D-dimension score that is structurally low (0/15) even though contractual data-handling controls likely exist for SOC 2/HIPAA-scoped engagements.

The ToS v1.1 Benchmarking Restriction is the most distinctive transparency-relevant clause observed in this evaluation. It creates a documented tension with the operator's public LoCoMo leadership positioning: third parties evaluating Zep Cloud under the standard ToS cannot publish comparative results without prior written consent. The methodology dispute filed publicly on getzep/zep-papers (Mem0 CTO, recalculating to 58.44% ± 0.20) is on the open-source paper repository rather than against the commercial product per se, and remains open at evaluation date. The data shows operator-published benchmark numbers and externally contested protocol; readers can navigate the open repository issue and the independent academic papers (Rasmussen et al., 2025 on arXiv; multiple third-party LoCoMo evaluations in the 2025–2026 academic literature) directly.

The corporate identity disclosure pattern — "Zep Software, Inc." in the contractual Cover Page, "Zep AI, Inc." in the website copyright, no postal address on the operator's own About page, and disagreement between Tracxn (Oakland) and CB Insights (San Francisco) — is a minor data point in isolation but adds to the broader picture of a 2.3M-cumulative-funding seed-stage operator (per CB Insights public record) whose enterprise-grade security investments outpace its public legal-disclosure investments.
